Why the gate needs two signals, not one

The cross-encoder is an excellent ranker and a badly calibrated absolute scorer: on answerable questions its median is 0.977 but its 10th percentile is 0.000, because it scores correct-but-paraphrased passages near zero. Cosine similarity survives paraphrase but rates topically-adjacent irrelevant chunks highly. Gating on either alone is unusable. Requiring both to be low cuts false refusals from 30% to 13% — and the held-out number below is the honest counterweight: on ten unanswerable questions written after the threshold was fixed, deliberately reusing in-domain vocabulary, it caught one.

What this is. A browser port of the retrieval half of a hybrid RAG service. The Python original adds a local Ollama model for answer generation with inline citations; generation is deliberately not shipped here, because a 3B model would be a 2 GB download and retrieval is the part worth watching anyway.

What is real. The chunker, BM25 (including rank_bm25's negative-IDF epsilon floor), the fusion arithmetic, the cross-encoder and the gate thresholds are ports of the shipped code. Models are the int8-quantised ONNX builds of the same checkpoints, so embeddings agree with the PyTorch originals to about three decimal places rather than exactly.

What is not claimed. The eval numbers come from a 35-question set over a 3-document corpus. They are meaningful as relative comparisons between configurations, not as absolute quality scores — and the abstention threshold demonstrably does not generalise well.
